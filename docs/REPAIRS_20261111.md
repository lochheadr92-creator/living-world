## Findings

Classification: **defect** (a rule or path that produces something the world could not have produced), **expected hardship**, **missing
capability**, **misleading observer output**, **null** (checked and not a problem), **unresolved**.

### 1. A grave dug in a doorway shut the household in for good (defect, repaired: rules 190)

Symptom: meadow, day 23.9, Mona dies of hunger at the Hazel hut's door; Sofia and Kai inside are refused "home exit is blocked" from then on,
and both die of thirst behind it (ticks 59,099 and 60,615). Birch, day 22.9, Hugo (not a member) dies of exposure at the Rowan hut's door; Gus inside is refused
from tick 55,100 and dies of thirst at tick 58,981. Cause: `killPerson` marks a grave on the nearest free land tile within five tiles, the door
tile is free land, a grave is solid for good (`registry.ts registerGeneric`), and `leaveHome` and `canEnterHome` require the door tile walkable.
Scope: every rich world from rules 3 (habitation) on, whenever someone dies within five tiles of a home's door with the door the nearest free
tile; deaths at doors are common in a cold snap. Repair: `src/sim/grave_site.ts`, rules 190; record `docs/GRAVE_SITE.md`.

### 2. The feed named a household on a site that belonged to nobody (misleading observer output, repaired: rules 191)

Symptom: "Dax marked out a storehouse for the Hazel household" beside "Dax proposed a shared storehouse for Willowbank" at the same tick, the
site public from the tick it existed (end-to-end trace, link 4; the same for a well). Cause: the one feed line in `act_build.ts` took the
actor's household unless the activity carried the communal flag, after `proposeProject` had already made the site public. Repair:
`src/sim/layout_words.ts`, rules 191; record `docs/LAYOUT_WORDS.md`.

### 3. The first storehouse was wanted from the world's register (defect, actor-local knowledge; repaired: rules 192)

Symptom: eight people in aspen choose "Lay out a storehouse" within a tenth of a day of a hut being finished whether or not they had seen it;
the want reopens for everyone the moment a site is cancelled (trace links 2, 9, 18, 21, 24). Cause: `firstStorehouseWanted` read
`world.buildings` and `noFirstStorehouse` read the world's buildings and sites (gap G32, gated until 10 November on held files). Repair: the
cue is now a believed solid home and no believed storehouse, and the reason says why now (`src/sim/shared_stone.ts`, rules 192); record
`docs/KNOWN_FIRST_STOREHOUSE.md`. The rest of G32 (own-site, open-site cap, the non-township huts count) is not taken.

### 4. Exposure deaths in the first cold snap (expected hardship; one capability gap for the owner)

43 of 187 deaths. Nearly all at warmth 0 to 5, energy 0, outdoors, in rain or storm at -2 to -13 C around day 21 to 23 (and day 10 to 13 in
cedar, fern), while already doing the thing the rules offer: "Sheltering at the lean-to", "Warming up by the fire", "Drinking". A lean-to gives
a warmth bonus of 5 and cover 0.55 (`needs.ts shelterAt`); at -6 C in rain the equilibrium warmth is below the death line, so a lean-to does
not save anyone and the rules say so. Gus (gen-5) died of exposure inside a hut at condition 20, which protects at half strength by design.
Not a defect: the decisions were the right ones for the knowledge and the options. The gap behind it is owner-level: a person may shelter only
in their own household's home (`habitation.ts canEnterHome`, "no guests in this slice"; ledger G70, G87), so in aspen two huts for 25 people
let nobody else in during the snap. Whether anyone may take shelter under another household's roof is a world rule for the owner; the 4
November survival brainstorm lists huddling. Nothing built.

### 5. Hunger deaths in the famine (expected hardship; one knowledge consequence worth knowing about)

63 deaths, nearly all with "hungry +85" at a remembered source that "has paid me poorly before" or that they had found empty, in a world
where food beliefs are optimistic (the rules-161 diagnosis). One traced case, gen-2: Ivo, 10, died of hunger on day 29.4 ten tiles from his
household's hut, which held 10 food from day 28.4 (`child_probe.ts`). He had last seen the store empty on day 27.0 (belief 0, 4,777 ticks
old), ranged 30 to 40 tiles for food, and never went home again; his carer Kira never had him in sight in those two days, tried once to
bring him berries on a sight from tick 64,740, then lost him, and her own belief of the store after depositing eight units was 1 (the rules-169
glance). Honest knowledge on both sides, no rule misfiring; the rules-169 "household reading its own store as nearly empty" question is the
owner's open line in `loops.md`. Not repaired.

### 6. Two alike promises held at once (null)

gen-1 and gen-4 showed one person holding two active "deliver 2 berries to <carer>" commitments. Traced (`dup_probe.ts`, gen-1, Una): the first
was promised at tick 22,904 for a care request, 1 of 2 delivered, overdue past 24,704 with grace; the carer asked again at 26,988 and Una
promised again. A renewed ask after an overdue, half-kept promise is reasonable, and the answer rule's two-commitment cap allows it. Not a defect.

### 7. A promise to someone who has died stays active (null)

33 such commitments in aspen, 55 in fern. By design since rules 170 and the promise-expectations rule (`social.ts socialOnDeath`): the
promise is made moot only when the promiser has learned the death. Not a defect.

Aspen, birch, gen-4: a drink or gather held for 1,300 ticks with that block. A remembered flood that has receded holds the person until they
see the ground again. Not traced further; no death was found behind it.
