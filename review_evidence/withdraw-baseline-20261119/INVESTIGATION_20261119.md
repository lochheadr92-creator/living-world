## Withdrawal observer

The observer samples active withdrawals before each world step and only creates its record there (`scripts/observers/withdraw_check.ts`). The end hook records an unseen ending if no record exists. An activity can start and end during a single world step: `startActivity` emits the start hook and immediately invokes `beginWork` for a work-phase activity; a refused `begin` ends it before the next sample (`src/sim/activities.ts`). This is a supported mechanism for missing a short activity, but the recorded River event has not been independently shown to follow that exact start path. The previously reproduced event is an inaccessible cellar doorway with zero moved; its age and unobserved departure information remain unknown. Do not manufacture those fields or mark the original aggregate check as passed.

Classification: observer coverage defect, with exact cause of the recorded event still to verify. Candidate: capture start-hook data as well as sampled data, explicitly marking which fields were sampled versus known only at a hook. Test same-step start/end, pre-existing activity, hook chaining and no double-counting, then replay River and compare watched/unwatched hashes and conservation. This proposal has not been implemented.

## Age-stage walking check

`TripShape.core` increments its independent age count using each person's age at every sampled tick, while each trip is assigned a stage once at its first walking tick and all its ticks are reported under that stage (`scripts/observers/trip_shape.ts`). `ageYears` changes with world tick. A person turning twelve during a walk can therefore produce a per-stage discrepancy even when all walking ticks reconcile. This is a code-supported hypothesis, not yet a verified explanation of the failing saved runs; other causes remain possible. Quantify discrepancies and inspect the relevant person and trip records before changing the observer. Any repair must choose and label either per-tick age or age at trip start, and keep the two denominators consistent.

## Water

The saved stage-7 Fern rules-255 report records 180 thirst deaths in its 96-day continuation. The independent replay at `review_evidence/stage7-20261117/probe-draw-on.txt` shows 17 wells with empty groundwater and lake stock falling from 210 at day 144.2 to zero at day 145; the population fell from 179 at day 145 to zero at day 148. This is one historical trajectory, not evidence of current rules-257 population rates. The off replay and original analysis are documented in `docs/GROWTH_PLAN.md`.

Source tracing: each well has up to 240 groundwater units, gains rain only, and initially finds 120 (`groundwater.ts`); its visible store holds 12 and refills at most one unit per 50 ticks (`wells.ts`). Surface water is also replenished by rain and loses water to draws and evaporation (`hydrology.ts`). Well demand is raised when nearby remembered wells are all empty and recently seen, rather than by a groundwater reserve forecast (`wellDemand`). Natural-water beliefs already distinguish wet, low and dry; low and dry influence the remembered choice cost and fade with age (`water_access.ts`). Thus saying there is no response at all to low water would be inaccurate; whether that existing response reaches the right people soon enough in Fern is unmeasured.

Next investigation: locate the original Fern time-series saves, compare water stocks, refill, draw and weather over time with other available worlds, and inspect what people saw and did before depletion. Distinguish total physical stock from well throughput and accessible/remembered water. Do not infer that adding wells, household storage, a warning or an arrival restriction solves the failure without a controlled comparison.
