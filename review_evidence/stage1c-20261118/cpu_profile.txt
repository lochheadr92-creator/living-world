WHAT THE TWO PROFILES SAY
  1. Time is in the people, not in the five systems. stepPerson holds 85% of the time (vite-node profile). Perception and belief upkeep
     are the heavy part: perception.ts 15.2% self, knowledge.ts 9.2%, handover_state.ts 6.9% (personalHandovers alone 6.5% to 7.1%),
     options_work.ts 6.3%; observe 14.6% inclusive, personalHandovers 6.9% inclusive. In the bundled profile the top function is
     observe (9.0%), then personalHandovers (7.1%), snapshotEntity (4.6%), describeSeenPerson (4.6%).
  2. The random generator is a rounding error. rng.ts self time is 0.02% (vite-node). Calls to next() are probably inlined by the engine
     (LIKELY, not checked), in which case their time is counted under the callers, so 0.02% is a lower bound on the share of the draws;
     but the callers themselves are nearly absent from the profile (item 3), so the draws cannot be large.
  3. The five systems of step 5 are about 0.2% of the time together, inclusive: updateEnvironment 0.02%, updateSources 0.16% (this holds
     all of the tree and berry upkeep, not only the seeding), spoilGoods 0.01%, immigrationTick about 0, the wild grain gather about 0.
     Those functions run rarely (spoilage every 120 ticks, an arrival check every 400, a tree seed check every 240 per tree), so
     the draws they make are few: spoilage draws once per perishable unit every 120 ticks (about 1,400 units of food in store on this
     day, so tens of thousands of draws a day, LIKELY), the others far fewer. A keyed draw costs a hash where a draw costs a generator
     step, about the same order of work (LIKELY; step 5 times it). Keyed draws are not a CPU question.
  4. No code change comes from this step. The finding for later: if speed ever matters, the lever is perception and belief upkeep at
     150 or more people (observe, snapshotEntity, describeSeenPerson, personalHandovers, refreshBeliefs, gridQuery).

LIMITS
  One world, one day, one run each way. The step 4 runs (18 arms) were running at the same time and the machine was at about 93% CPU
  load, so times are slower than a quiet machine; shares are less affected but not unaffected. Sampling is the default interval.
  Self time counts a function's own body only: a function inlined into its caller is counted under the caller.
