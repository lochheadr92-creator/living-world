## Question 2, the finding with a mechanism
**How big.** Withdraws that took none, empty: 959 of 2,495 (aspen), 519 of 1,764 (river), 1,226 of 3,057 (gen-4), 29 to 40% of all adult withdraws and 36 to 54% of the walking ticks spent on withdraws, 2.2 to 4.1% of all adult walking ticks. The bakery is the largest single case: withdraws whose goal is "to bake the household's grain into bread, which feeds further and keeps" (bread from the bakery, flour from the mill, wood from the timber yard) are 55%, 40% and 49% of all adult withdraws. Aspen: 876 such walks, 53 took bread, 665 came back empty, the person believed 1 loaf was there (mean 0.99) and the bakery held 4.2 on average. Other workshops follow the same pattern, in smaller numbers (kiln, smithy, mill, quarry, food rack, timber yard). (VERIFIED)

**Why (VERIFIED by reading, and measured in wave 2).** Planning counts the workshop's output as stock for anyone who may use the workshop: `production.ts collectOptions` (lines 551 to 557) calls `site_supply.ts benchStock` (84 to 87, the rules 229 "bench" part), which reads `b.ops.output` from the person's snapshot of the workshop and does not ask whom it was ordered by. At the transfer, `act_resource.ts` withdraw `work()` (843 to 893) takes goods through `facilities.ts withdrawAllowance` (202 to 217) and `usableUnits` (114 to 122): stock earmarked to the person, plus stock that is not earmarked when the person's access is owner, communal or builder. Finished goods are earmarked (`kind: 'out'`, `owner: client`) to whoever ordered the batch (`facilities.ts` 647, 661, 670). So a person who did not order the batch sees bread waiting, walks to it, and is allowed none. Wave 2 read the sim's own access functions on the last work tick before each transfer: for the withdraws that arrived at stock and were allowed none, the stock was earmarked to other people in 96.3, 98.4 and 64.5% of cases, and only 35, 21 and 37 of those had an owner in the person's own household (so almost all were another household's batch). In 907, 505 and 1,116 of the 959, 519 and 1,226 empty withdraws the person's own snapshot showed output waiting; in only 18, 4 and 10 did it name the person as the batch's client. In gen-4, 414 of 1,168 were guest stock, 407 of them at a timber yard: the person's access was friend or guest level at another household's workplace, whose own stock is not open to them (rules 207, `open_workplaces.ts`; the building's household was not recorded for these, so "another household's" is the access level, not a lookup). Granary shares and "no access" explain none of it (0, 0 and 1 granary; 0 no access).

**The repeat.** 99% of the second and later empty walks to a place were by a different person than the last (aspen 932 of 944, river 502 of 506, gen-4 1,194 of 1,208). The sim already remembers a failure for 320 ticks (`production.ts collectLeaf` 169 to 174, `knowledge.ts recentFailure`), but the memory is the person's own, so every resident learns separately. (VERIFIED for the counts; the link to the personal memory is my reading of the code.)

**Not stale belief.** The belief was the glance of rules 169 in 67 to 69% of all withdraws (one of each kind where more was there), but that mattered little here: the stock was there. The belief said less than the truth in 1,721, 1,178 and 2,104 withdraws and more than the truth in only 87, 100 and 155. (VERIFIED)

## Question 1, loads

Of the gathers that did their job, 39, 46 and 39% began with room for 2 units or fewer of the item taken. About 29 to 34% of the tiny gathers had room and took less than planned (the place gave less than the person's plan, which comes from the person's belief of what the source holds); about 10% had room and a small plan. ## Question 3, dropped walks
Walks that ended before arrival hold 19.4 to 20.6% of all adult walking ticks (aspen 762,314 of 3,761,282 over the three controls, river 880,016 of 4,525,137, gen-4 953,763 of 4,638,463). Gather and withdraw are 39 to 42% of that; the rest is deposit, explore, flee, give and operate. Of the gather and withdraw drop ticks, "something better came up" and "switched to something else" are 68 to 77%, "the place looked empty" 15 to 16% (the found-empty rule of rules 161), danger 7 to 16%. The largest single pairing is a gather replaced by another gather: 106,487, 137,511 and 156,281 ticks (14 to 16% of all dropped ticks). A review happens every 45 ticks plus up to 19 and a new best option wins by a factor of 1.3 plus 3 once the minimum commitment is over (`decision.ts reviewActivity`, `constants.ts`). (VERIFIED for the counts.) A dropped walk is not all waste: a switch to something nearer or more urgent is the review working as designed. How much of the 20% is waste is UNKNOWN; this run counts ticks, not what they bought.

Also seen, outside the three questions: walks that arrived and did nothing hold 7.4 to 10.4% of adult walking ticks. The largest named causes: `operate` failed "lacks # flour" (aspen 51,120 ticks) and "lacks # wood" (gen-4 130,382 ticks, 2.8% of adult walking), and `gather` failed "nothing left" or "it is gone" (1.7 to 2.3%). ## What this says about the lever (LIKELY, my arithmetic from the figures above)
As shares of all awake person-ticks (all ages; adult walking share of awake ticks 0.36 to 0.43, walking share of all awake ticks 42.6 to 50.5%): empty withdraws about 0.8 to 1.7%; gathers of 2 units or fewer about 3.2 to 3.3% (their walking, not their saving); dropped walks about 6.8 to 8.8%; arrived with no job about 2.4 to 4.8%. These overlap and are not savings: a walk that is not made is mostly replaced by another. The life gap in the earlier runs is 15 to 18% against a proposed 30%. So the walking waste is real, countable and in two cases a plain rule mismatch, but none of the three pieces alone is near the size of that gap, and the first (the earmark mismatch) is the smallest in time and the clearest in cause. Whether fixing any of them moves life time is UNKNOWN. The upper bound run (UE) moved life 5 to 7 points by taking stocking away; nothing here has been tried on the walking.
